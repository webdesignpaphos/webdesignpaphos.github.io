Your About page rarely closes a sale on its own, but it frequently decides whether a visitor stays long enough for anything else to close it. Research consistently puts the About page in the top two or three most visited pages on a typical business website, often second only to the homepage. Yet most About pages are full of corporate boilerplate, stock photography, and vague mission statements that tell visitors nothing useful.

This guide walks through what makes an About page work from a design and UX standpoint: the sections that build credibility, the visual decisions that keep people reading, and the mistakes that send visitors straight back to Google.

## Why Your About Page Matters More Than You Think

Most business owners treat the About page as an afterthought, updating it once every few years and filling it with a brief history of the company and a headshot from 2018. That is a missed opportunity.

Visitors land on your About page at a specific moment in their decision-making journey: they are already interested enough in what you offer to want to know more about who is behind it. They are asking questions like: Are these people credible? Do I like how they communicate? Can I trust them with my project or money?

A well-designed About page answers those questions quickly and clearly. A poorly designed one leaves visitors uncertain and more likely to check out a competitor.

## The Elements a High-Converting About Page Needs

There is no single template that works for every business, but a handful of elements appear consistently across About pages that perform well.

### A Clear and Human Opening

The first thing visitors should see is a statement that immediately conveys who you are, what you do, and who you serve. Skip "We are a team of passionate professionals dedicated to delivering excellence." Nobody believes that.

Instead, open with something specific and honest. A small agency might write: "We build websites for service businesses that want more than a nice-looking brochure online. We focus on speed, clarity, and getting you enquiries."

That kind of opening tells a visitor in one sentence whether they are in the right place.

From a design standpoint, this opening works best as a large, bold headline with a short supporting paragraph beneath it. Pair it with a genuine photo of your team or workspace, not a stock image of people in suits shaking hands.

### Your Origin Story

The story section is where you explain why the business exists. Visitors do not need a timeline of every milestone since you launched. They need to understand your motivation and your point of view.

Keep this section focused. Three to five short paragraphs are usually enough. Write in a conversational tone that reflects how your team actually speaks. If you can explain the specific problem you set out to solve, or the gap you noticed in the market, that is far more compelling than a generic "we founded this company to help businesses grow."

Use real language, specific details, and avoid phrases that every competitor also uses.

From a layout perspective, this section often works well in a two-column arrangement: story on the left, a strong supporting image on the right. On mobile, the two columns stack vertically, with the image dropping below the text.

### Team Photos and Real People

One of the most consistent findings in user research on trust is that showing real people behind a business significantly increases credibility. A photo of a smiling team member next to their name and role makes the business feel human and accountable.

Resist the urge to skip this section. Even if your business is a solo operation, a professional headshot and a short paragraph about your background goes a long way.

For team sections, grid layouts with cards work well: photo, name, role, and optionally a short one-liner about the person. Use consistent photo styles. All photos should have similar lighting, cropping, and background treatment. Mixing a formal studio portrait with a casual phone photo taken in a kitchen creates visual noise that undermines credibility.

For businesses in Cyprus, where many clients still value personal relationships before they sign any contract, showing the actual people they will be working with is particularly important.

### Social Proof and Trust Signals

Once visitors have read your story and seen your team, they want third-party confirmation that you are as good as you say you are. This is where social proof earns its place.

**Client testimonials** are the highest-value trust signal. A short, specific quote from a client, with their full name, job title, and company, carries far more weight than a star rating or a logo. Aim for two to four testimonials on the About page. Pick quotes that address the specific concerns a hesitant prospect might have: quality, communication, turnaround time, results.

**Logos of past clients or partners** provide quick visual credibility. A small, clean row of recognisable logos tells visitors you have worked with real organisations. Do not include dozens of logos: five to eight is enough.

**Certifications, awards, or press mentions** add weight if they are relevant to what you sell. A Google Partner badge, an industry award, or a mention in a respected publication all signal credibility. Display them simply and without clutter.

From a design standpoint, keep the social proof section visually lighter than the rest of the page. Testimonial cards with a subtle background, a small headshot, and clean typography work well. Avoid heavy borders, drop shadows, or decorative quote marks that feel dated.

### A Clear Call to Action

Every section of your About page should point toward a next step, and the end of the page must have a specific call to action that tells the visitor exactly what to do next.

The most common options are:

- "View our work" (pointing to a portfolio or case studies page)
- "See our services" (pointing to a services overview)
- "Get in touch" (pointing to a contact form or phone number)

The right CTA depends on your business and where your visitors are in their decision process. If most About page visitors already know what you offer and just needed a credibility check, "Get in touch" is usually the correct choice. If visitors might not yet know the full scope of what you do, sending them to a services page first can improve the quality of enquiries you receive.

Design the CTA button with clear contrast, generous padding, and a label that describes the action rather than just saying "Click here." A button that reads "Start your project" is more compelling and more informative than a generic "Contact us."

## Layout and Visual Design Principles

### Establish a Visual Rhythm

About pages that feel professional are usually built with a consistent visual rhythm: alternating sections of content with photography, consistent spacing between headings and body text, and a clear typographic hierarchy. Headings should be noticeably larger than body text. Subheadings should sit comfortably between the two.

Use consistent padding between sections. On desktop, 80px to 120px of vertical space between major sections keeps the page feeling open without creating excessive scrolling. On mobile, reduce this to 48px to 64px.

### Choose Photography That Reflects Reality

Nothing undermines an About page faster than stock photography. Visitors have seen those same images of laptops on coffee tables and diverse teams in open-plan offices on hundreds of other websites. They carry zero credibility.

Invest in a half-day photography session. Get shots of your team at work, your workspace or studio, and candid behind-the-scenes moments. Even a smartphone with good lighting can produce usable images. What matters is that the photography is genuine.

If a photoshoot is not immediately possible, use illustrated or abstract visuals rather than stock people. A clean abstract background with your brand colours looks more honest than a staged corporate photo.

### Use Colour to Signal Personality

Your About page is an appropriate place to let your brand's personality come through more than on a product or services page. A slightly warmer colour palette, a more personal tone in the copy, and a layout that feels more editorial than transactional all work in your favour here.

That does not mean abandoning your brand guidelines. It means applying them in a way that feels human rather than mechanical.

### Typography Choices

Body text on an About page should be set at a comfortable reading size: 17px to 19px on desktop, 16px on mobile. Line spacing of 1.6 to 1.7 keeps long paragraphs readable without feeling airy.

Use one or two typefaces at most. A strong serif for headings and a clean sans-serif for body text is a combination that communicates both substance and approachability. Avoid decorative display fonts unless they are core to your brand identity.

## Mobile Design Considerations

More than half of all web traffic comes from mobile devices, and About pages are no exception. A page that looks impressive on a 1440px monitor but collapses into an unreadable mess on an iPhone has failed at its job.

Test every section in a mobile view before publishing. Key things to check:

- Team cards should stack vertically, not shrink to unreadable sizes
- Hero images should crop sensibly on portrait screens
- CTA buttons should span the full width of the mobile viewport for easy tapping
- Body text should never require horizontal scrolling

Keep tap targets large. Buttons and links should be at least 44px tall to meet accessibility guidelines and reduce accidental taps.

## Page Performance

A slow About page hurts credibility before a visitor reads a single word. If your page takes more than three seconds to load on a mobile connection, a significant portion of visitors will leave before they see your story or team.

Compress all images before uploading. AVIF and WebP formats offer substantially smaller file sizes than JPEG and PNG at equivalent visual quality. An uncompressed 3MB team photo can often be reduced to under 150KB without any visible quality loss.

Limit custom fonts to two weights per typeface. Each additional font weight is a separate network request. Loading four weights of a custom typeface when you only use two on the page is unnecessary overhead.

Use browser-native lazy loading on images that appear below the fold: `loading="lazy"` on any `<img>` tag that is not visible on initial page load. This defers image loading until the visitor scrolls to that section, improving perceived load speed and Largest Contentful Paint scores.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Writing in the third person throughout.** "Our team has 15 years of experience helping clients achieve their goals" is harder to connect with than "We have spent 15 years helping our clients." Third person creates distance; first person creates a relationship.

**Burying the CTA at the very bottom.** If your page is long, add a secondary CTA midway through. Some visitors will be ready to act long before they finish reading.

**Using outdated team photos.** A team photo from several years ago, especially if team members have since left or changed roles, creates an awkward disconnect if a client meets different people in person.

**Ignoring page load time.** A beautiful About page that takes six seconds to load on a phone has the opposite effect of what you intend.

**Forgetting to update the page.** Your About page should be a living document. If your team has grown, if you have completed notable projects, if your focus has shifted, the page should reflect that. Schedule a quarterly review.

## Putting It Together

The best About pages feel honest, specific, and personal without trying too hard. They give visitors enough information to feel confident, enough social proof to feel reassured, and a clear next step to act on.

From a design perspective, the goal is a page that is easy to scan, visually coherent, and fast to load on any device. Every section should earn its place by answering a question the visitor is already asking.

Start with your opening statement. Make it specific and human. Add your story in plain language. Show your team with real photography. Include two to four strong testimonials. Finish with a single, clear call to action.

Then test it on your phone. If it does not make you want to get in touch with your own business, keep editing.
